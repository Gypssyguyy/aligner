Step-by-Step OS Setup & Calibration Guide

How to apply these calculated alignment coordinates in your operating system.

1

Open Display Settings

Right-click anywhere on your Desktop and select Display Settings (or press Win + I → System → Display).

2

Identify Display Boxes

Click Identify to verify which monitor number corresponds to your laptop screen (usually Monitor 1) and your desktop screen (Monitor 2).

3

Drag to Calculated Offset

Click and drag the Monitor box vertically up or down. Position it according to the calculated physical height alignment shown above, then click Apply.

Pro Tip for Windows Users: Windows arranges monitors strictly based on total virtual render resolution (not physical screen size in inches). If you have a high-DPI 4K display next to a 1080p laptop display, lowering display scaling or matching physical PPI reduces cursor jump distance drastically.
1

Open System Settings

Click the Apple icon in the top-left corner, select System Settings, and navigate to Displays.

2

Click "Arrange..."

Click the Arrange... button at the top to open the visual monitor alignment blueprint canvas.

3

Fine-Nudge Display Window

Drag your external display icon up or down until the top or bottom edges align physically with your MacBook screen edge. Click Done.

MacBook Stand Height Factor: If your laptop sits on an elevated aluminum cooling stand, measure the physical height difference from desk surface to bottom bezel with a ruler, then add that physical offset into the alignment slider.
